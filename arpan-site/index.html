Why I do this

A voice, a dark room, and no way to look away.

If a line does not hold, the listener leaves. Still the only rule I trust

Every Sunday evening the house went quiet for an hour. A story came out of a speaker and nobody moved. No screen, no subtitles, nothing to look at, just a voice and whatever your own head built around it.

I did not know then that writing was doing all of that work. I only knew I wanted to be on the other side of the speaker. Years later I got there, and found that radio drama is the strictest school there is. There is no picture to hide behind. A weak line has nowhere to go. Lose someone and you do not get a second attempt at the same sentence, they are simply gone.

Economics gave me the other half. I liked that attention leaves a trace you can actually read, that a feeling can be counted. A dashboard now tells me what a Sunday evening silence used to tell me: whether anyone stayed.

So a mutual fund explainer and a Sherlock adaptation turn out to be the same problem in different clothes. Someone is one swipe from leaving, and the only thing holding them is the next line. That is the part I am here for. Not the deliverable. The moment a stranger forgets they were about to scroll.

About

The short version.

I started by adapting Bengali novels for radio, which is a strict school. There is no picture to lean on, so if a line does not hold, a listener leaves and you find out.

That habit carried into everything since. For Sunday Suspense and Goppo Mir-er Thek I turned novels and Conan Doyle into production ready Bengali audio scripts. At Grown Up Tales I made mutual funds and Form 16 into short videos people finished. At SVF I wrote for film audiences that changed completely from one title to the next. Now I run content for Insights.market inside the INVESMATE Group, where the feedback is a dashboard instead of a comment section, and where every claim has to clear SEBI compliance before it goes out.

I completed my masters in Economics at the University of Calcutta, which is mostly why I trust a chart over a hunch.
